## Rajshahi Travel Guide 2026: Everything You Need to Know

Rajshahi is Bangladesh’s fourth-largest city and one of its most rewarding destinations — yet it remains far less visited than Dhaka, Cox’s Bazar, or Sylhet. That’s exactly what makes it special. Known as the “Silk City” and the “City of Mangoes,” Rajshahi sits on the north bank of the Padma River and offers a remarkable combination of history, culture, nature, and food — all in a city that still feels genuinely local.

This 2026 travel guide covers everything you need to plan a perfect Rajshahi trip: what to see, where to eat, when to visit, how to get around — and why hiring a private car with a local driver makes every minute count.

## Best Time to Visit Rajshahi

**October to February** is the peak season — cool, dry weather makes sightseeing comfortable. This is when most tourists visit. **March to May** is hot but exciting: mango season begins in late April and peaks in May–June, drawing food lovers to the orchards of Chapai Nawabganj. **June to September** (monsoon) sees heavy rainfall, but the Padma is dramatic and the countryside lush.

## Top Attractions in Rajshahi

### 1\. Varendra Research Museum

Founded in 1910, the Varendra Research Museum is the oldest museum in Bangladesh. It holds over 9,000 artefacts including Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain sculptures, terracotta pieces, coins, and manuscripts spanning more than 2,000 years of South Asian history. The museum is a 15-minute walk or 5-minute drive from the city centre. Entry fee: ৳20 for adults.

### 2\. Puthia Temple Complex

Located 32 km east of Rajshahi, Puthia Upazila contains Bangladesh’s largest concentration of ancient Hindu temples — 23 temples built between the 17th and 19th centuries. The Shiva Temple, Govinda Temple, and Annik Ratna Temple are the most architecturally significant. There is no regular bus service direct to Puthia, making a rental car the most practical option.

### 3\. Rajshahi University Campus

One of Bangladesh’s most beautiful university campuses, spread over 753 acres of parkland, ponds, and century-old trees. The campus is particularly beautiful in February during spring, when flame trees bloom orange along the main avenue. Free to visit; best by car to cover the full grounds.

### 4\. Padma River Embankment (Badhaghat)

The riverfront promenade at Badhaghat is Rajshahi’s most popular evening destination. Watch fishermen work, take a boat ride on the Padma, eat grilled hilsa from riverside stalls, and watch the sun set over India across the river. Best visited between 4–7 PM.

### 5\. Rajshahi Silk & Mango Markets

Shaheb Bazaar is the main commercial area where silk sarees, fabrics, and local crafts are sold. The wholesale silk market near the railway station offers even better prices. During mango season, the Kansat Mango Market in Chapai Nawabganj (40 km west) is the largest mango wholesale market in Asia — a unique experience even if you’re not buying.

### 6\. Bagha Mosque

Built in 1523 during the Sultanate period, the Bagha Mosque (40 km from Rajshahi) is one of the finest examples of terracotta mosque architecture in Bengal. Intricate floral and geometric patterns cover the entire facade. A UNESCO tentative World Heritage Site and essential for history enthusiasts.

### 7\. Somapura Mahavihara (Paharpur)

Bangladesh’s most important archaeological site — a 9th-century Buddhist monastery covering 27 acres, with a central temple and 177 monks’ cells arranged around a courtyard. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1985, located 75 km north of Rajshahi. Allow a full day for this trip.

## Where to Eat in Rajshahi

**Ilish (Hilsa) fish** from the Padma is Rajshahi’s most famous dish — many visitors say Padma hilsa is the finest in Bangladesh. Try it grilled or in mustard sauce at riverfront restaurants near Badhaghat. **Rajshahi mangoes** (Langra, Fazli, Khirsapat) are available May–August from street stalls and the fruit market. **Local mishti (sweets)**, particularly rasmalai and chamcham from Shaheb Bazaar sweet shops, are outstanding.

## Getting to Rajshahi

-   **By Air:** Shah Makhdum Airport (RJH) receives daily flights from Dhaka (35–40 minutes). Biman Bangladesh and US-Bangla Airlines operate this route. Book 2–3 days ahead during peak season.
-   **By Train:** Silk City Express and Padma Express connect Dhaka Kamalapur with Rajshahi in 5–6 hours. Air-conditioned chair cars and berths available.
-   **By Road:** Luxury coaches (Hanif, Shyamoli) operate Dhaka–Rajshahi in 5–6 hours via the Bangabandhu Bridge. AC coaches leave from Gabtoli bus terminal.

## Rajshahi Travel Tips

-   Rajshahi is significantly hotter than Dhaka April–September. Carry water and wear light cotton clothing.
-   Most major sites are closed on Sundays (government holiday). Plan your itinerary around this.
-   The Rajshahi currency exchange rate for USD is similar to Dhaka. ATMs are widely available on Sahib Bazaar Road.
-   English is less commonly spoken here than in Dhaka — having a local driver who speaks English is a practical advantage.
