Design audit · dev.rajshahirentacar.bd · 5 October 2026

Built well. Looks like everyone else. Booking is broken.

The engineering underneath is sound: no layout breaks at 360 px, zero layout shift, accessibility at 96, Brotli compression on. The problems sit in the layer customers actually see. The site is assembled from one card pattern repeated about twenty times, nothing in it says Rajshahi, and on dev every booking fails while telling the customer their internet is down.

Fix these first

What is broken, wrong, or slow today

Ordered by what costs you bookings. Each was checked on the live dev site, then traced to the code. Resolved during the audit: all 13 blog posts were translated into Bangla (commit 75da6d9) and are live on dev.

  1. Broken

    Every booking fails, and the message blames the customer

    /api/booking returns 404, so the form always ends on “পাঠানো যায়নি — ইন্টারনেটে সমস্যা হয়েছে”. Someone with perfect signal is told their internet is the problem. Production will behave the same unless the api/ service is deployed.

    DoDeploy the booking service. Until then, send the form straight to WhatsApp and say so on the button. A server failure must never be described as the customer’s connection.

  2. Wrong

    None of the server header rules are applied

    public/.htaccess sets one-year immutable caching for JS and CSS plus security headers, but the dev server sends none of them. JavaScript arrives with no Cache-Control, images get a 7-day server default, and X-Content-Type-Options and Referrer-Policy are missing.

    DoFind out why the mod_headers block is skipped (the deployed file, or the <IfModule> wrapper on LiteSpeed), then confirm each header with curl -I.

  3. Wrong

    The ambulance card’s link says “পিকআপ-ড্রপ”

    Home page, “why choose us” grid. It goes to the ambulance page, but the tile prints COPY.services[2].label, and index 2 is the pickup service. In an emergency, a mislabelled link costs seconds.

    DoLabel it “এখনই কল করুন” and make it a direct call link.

  4. Wrong

    The email row shows a WhatsApp icon

    Contact page. The row links to mailto: but draws the WhatsApp glyph, so people expect a chat to open.

    DoUse an envelope icon for email.

  5. Wrong

    Route chips look like buttons and do nothing

    রাজশাহী → ঢাকা, নাটোর, পুঠিয়া… are shadowed pills with no link. People will tap them and assume the site is broken.

    DoMake each one open booking with the destination already filled in.

  6. Slow

    The header logo is 977 × 402 px and 212 KB

    Shown at about 90 px wide on every page, it is five times heavier than the hero photo and competes with it for bandwidth.

    DoExport at twice display size (about 180 px) as WebP, or redraw as SVG. Aim for under 10 KB.

  7. Slow

    Entrance animations hold back the main content

    Hero copy fades in with rise-in. On the contact page the largest element is a fading paragraph and it takes 4.1 s to count as shown.

    DoNothing in the first screen animates. Keep motion for below the fold, and sparingly.

  8. Polish

    Nothing is checked before the form is sent

    Both booking forms send whatever is typed, empty or not, straight to the server. A missing name or a five-digit number costs a network round trip, and while the server is missing it ends on the internet-problem message.

    DoCheck name and phone on the phone first, with Bangla messages under each field: “নাম লিখুন”, “সঠিক মোবাইল নম্বর দিন”.

  9. Polish

    The footer phone link is too small to tap

    Fails Lighthouse’s tap-target check on every page tested. The ambulance page also has a call button whose spoken label differs from its visible text.

    DoGive every tappable item at least a 44 × 44 px area, and start the spoken label with the visible words.

Why it reads as AI-made

Every decision is the default one

Nothing here is badly made. The issue is that each choice is the safe, common one, so the result looks like every other site assembled from the same parts. These are the six signals that do the most damage.

One card, about twenty times

An icon in a pale tinted square, a bold line, a grey sentence. It is the trust list, the “why choose us” grid, the services, the contact rows and the ambulance features. When one component carries everything, nothing stands out and the page has no hierarchy.

This is the strongest single signal of a generated site. Replace most of these with plain lists, large numbers, or photographs.

The stock car-rental hero

Darkened photo, price pill, headline, two buttons, a booking bar overlapping the bottom edge. It is the layout of most car-rental themes. It functions, and it says nothing a competitor’s site does not.

The car is also darkened until it stops being the subject. If the photo is worth showing, show it in full colour.

Nothing says Rajshahi

No Padma, no silk, no mango orchard, no local street. The three car photos come from three different places: one outdoors with a yellow number plate, one on a city street with a Bangla plate, one in a showroom with studio lights in the frame. Customers notice borrowed photos, and they notice inconsistency even when they can’t name it.

The grey band with a short green bar above every page title is another stock device. It appears on all inner pages.

Everything at the same volume

Seven screens on a phone, and no section is allowed to be bigger than the others. Pickup and ambulance each appear twice within two scrolls. The ambulance card switches to a pink palette that exists nowhere else on the site.

Premium sites edit. Fewer things, each given room, with one clear path to booking.

Decoration standing in for information

The route section has the most valuable content on the page, and it is reduced to five pills with no distance, no time and no price. Pills on every fleet card, a pulsing ring on the phone button and parallax drift on photos are the same kind of stock flourish.

Real numbers make better decoration than effects: পুঠিয়া ৩২ কিমি tells a customer more than a pin icon.

The logo says the name twice

The logo image has RAJSHAHI set inside it, and the Bangla name sits right beside it. At header size the English lettering is too small to read. Use the car-and-pin mark alone in the header, with the Bangla name as live text.

The premium direction

Grow up the brand you already have

Premium here doesn’t mean gold on black. It means confident, calm and specific: fewer elements, larger type, real photographs, and a booking flow that feels like a product. Build it from what is already yours: the deep green and red pin of your logo, your Bangla name, and Rajshahi itself.

  • Lead with the job, not a banner

    The first screen asks “কোথায় যাবেন?” and offers four trip types as large tiles. A 60-year-old and a 22-year-old both understand a tile.

  • One type family, used with range

    Anek Bangla and Anek Latin share a design and both have a width axis: wide and heavy for headlines, normal for reading, narrow for prices and distances. That retires Inter and one font file.

  • Colour taken from the logo

    Deep green for surfaces and the main button. The logo’s red is reserved for the pin: destinations, live status, emergency. No mint tints, amber buttons or pink cards.

  • Real numbers are the decoration

    Distances, travel times and per-day prices set large. A route board from Rajshahi says more than any icon grid.

  • Photograph the service, not the parking lot

    One shoot with one look: drivers, doors opening, the station, the Padma at dusk. Every car from the same angle in the same light.

  • Motion only where it means something

    Nothing moves in the first screen. One meaningful moment, such as a route line drawing in, instead of fades everywhere.

How this was checked

Method and limits

Speed and accessibility: Lighthouse 12 in mobile mode on six pages, simulated slow 4G on a 412 px mid-range phone.

Layout: horizontal overflow measured in a live browser at eleven widths from 320 to 1440 px, nine pages.

Behaviour: navigation, cards, links, the contact page and the booking form were checked by reading the live page, not the code. Response headers were read directly from the server.

Screenshots: Lighthouse’s full-page capture at 412 px and headless Chrome at 1440 px.

One thing I should have asked first: I submitted the quick-booking form once with test data (name রহিম, phone 12345). The booking service returned 404, so nothing was stored or sent.

Corrections: an earlier version of this report said an empty form showed an English browser message. Both forms set noValidate, so they send instead, which is the finding above. The blog finding was resolved by commit 75da6d9 while the audit was running.

Not covered: a real Android phone on a Bangladeshi network, and dark mode on the site.
